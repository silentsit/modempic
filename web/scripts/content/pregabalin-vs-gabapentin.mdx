Pregabalin vs gabapentin is a gabapentinoid comparison of two labeled products. Pregabalin is the US generic for [Lyrica](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=d4734e7d-5079-455e-8ff5-8f4539c998a9), a Schedule V prescription medicine with linear oral absorption. Gabapentin is the generic for [Neurontin](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ee9ad9ed-6d9f-4ee1-9d7f-cfad438df388). It is not federally scheduled. Its bioavailability falls as the daily dose rises.[1][2][3]

Neither drug is an opioid. Neither one binds GABA receptors, even though the names sound like they should.[1][2]

On 17 September 2026 this catalog listed one pregabalin SKU, [Nervigesic 300 mg](/product/buy-lyrica-pregabalin-nervigesic-300-mg), and zero gabapentin SKUs. This page is catalog and label education, not medical advice. Legal status varies by country.

## They are not GABA boosters

The Lyrica label states that pregabalin does not bind GABAA, GABAB, or benzodiazepine receptors, and does not change rat-brain GABA concentration or have acute effects on GABA uptake or degradation.[1]

The Neurontin label says gabapentin is structurally related to GABA but has no effect on GABA binding, uptake, or degradation.[2] Both bind the alpha2-delta subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels. The labels still call the full analgesic mechanism incompletely worked out.[1][2]

A page that ranked for this query on 17 September 2026 told readers the drugs "work to increase the amount of GABA (a pain-fighting transmitter)." That sentence is the opposite of the US prescribing information. The names are a chemistry leftover.

## Why one is Schedule V and the other is a state patchwork

The DEA placed pregabalin in Schedule V on 28 July 2005.[3] DailyMed still lists Lyrica as CV.[1] Gabapentin's Neurontin label lists DEA schedule as none.[2]

That split is a US fact. From 1 April 2019 the UK classified both pregabalin and gabapentin as Class C under the Misuse of Drugs Act 1971 and Schedule 3 under the Misuse of Drugs Regulations 2001.[8] Searchers typing "what is the difference between gabapentin and pregabalin NHS" are not looking at the DEA table.

Inside the US, gabapentin is the moving piece. A Temple University public-health law Q&A published 30 January 2026 described state scheduling and PDMP reporting that spread, then slowed. Michigan is the jurisdiction that scheduled gabapentin and later took it back off the controlled list.[9]

This page will not reprint a seven-state list from 2022 as if it were current. I do not have a board-by-board count I can defend today. Check the state board of pharmacy.

## Why 6:1 calculators fail at high gabapentin doses

Pharmacy Times and conversion blogs quote a rough 6:1 gabapentin-to-pregabalin milligram ratio. The ratio comes from conversion studies and from Bockbrader's pharmacokinetic comparison. It is not a labeled switch table.[6]

Gabapentin uptake saturates. At 900 mg/day about 60% of the oral dose is absorbed. At 3,600 mg/day that figure is about 33%. At 4,800 mg/day it is about 27%.[2]

Pregabalin stays at ≥90% across the labeled range.[1] A ratio fitted at one gabapentin dose does not stay true when the gut transporter is already full.

The Lyrica label also refuses a combo recipe. "The efficacy of adjunctive LYRICA in patients taking gabapentin has not been evaluated in controlled trials. Consequently, dosing recommendations for the use of LYRICA with gabapentin cannot be offered."[1]

This catalog cannot run a titration even if a prescriber wanted one. The only pregabalin SKU here is 300 mg. The US capsule list also includes 25, 50, 75, 100, 150, 200, and 225 mg.[1] Those strengths are not in this cart.

## The 2019 breathing warning the listicles skip

On 19 December 2019 the FDA warned that serious breathing problems can occur with gabapentin (Neurontin, Gralise, Horizant) or pregabalin (Lyrica, Lyrica CR) in people who also take opioids or other CNS depressants, who have reduced lung function such as COPD, or who are older.[4]

The agency required new respiratory-depression warnings on the labels. It also required manufacturers to run trials on abuse potential, especially with opioids.[4] Both labels now carry that warning.[1][2]

The consumer "7 differences" pages that ranked on 17 September 2026 spent their space on how fast each drug starts and which one is controlled. They did not lead with this.

"Is Lyrica an opioid" still draws thousands of US searches a month. The answer is no. The breathing risk with opioids is the part of that question the schedule table does not answer.

## Nerve pain evidence is indication-specific

A January 2025 systematic review in *Frontiers in Pain Research* pooled 14 studies and 3,346 patients with neuropathic pain. Pregabalin had a lower visual analog pain score than gabapentin (SMD −0.47, 95% CI −0.74 to −0.19), better quality-of-life scores, fewer days of severe pain, and lower odds of ongoing opioid use (OR 0.50, 95% CI 0.33–0.76). Nausea and vomiting were more common on gabapentin in that pool.[5]

Read the author line. María Pérez-Páramo is listed from the medical department of Viatris, Madrid. Viatris Specialty LLC is the packager on the DailyMed Lyrica label used here.[1][5] That does not void the statistics. It is a conflict a ranking synopsis should have named.

The same paper's abstract can be read as if renal adjustment were a gabapentin problem. Both labels include creatinine-clearance tables. Pregabalin doses in the Lyrica PI are already split by CrCl bands. Gabapentin's Neurontin PI says the same for reduced renal function.[1][2] Those tables are labeled facts for a clinician. They are not a regimen this page can pick.

Robertson and colleagues ran a different trial. Eighteen adults with chronic sciatica, one Australian neurosurgery clinic, randomized, double-blind, double-dummy crossover. Gabapentin reduced leg-pain intensity more and had fewer, less severe adverse events.[10]

That is not the 3,346-person neuropathic-pain pool. It still matters if sciatica is the diagnosis.

Diabetic nerve pain, postherpetic neuralgia, fibromyalgia, and spinal cord injury pain are different labeled jobs. Pregabalin has the extra US approvals for diabetic neuropathy, fibromyalgia, and spinal cord injury pain.[1] Gabapentin is often used off-label for some of those. Off-label is a prescribing decision.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is pregabalin stronger than gabapentin?

It is absorbed more completely, and Bockbrader's model estimated a lower EC50 in epilepsy data, which is a potency difference on that endpoint.[1][6] The 18-person chronic-sciatica crossover still found gabapentin reduced pain more in that sample.[10] "Stronger" depends on the condition and the dose the gut actually absorbs.

### Can you take pregabalin and gabapentin together?

No. The Lyrica label says combo use with gabapentin has not been evaluated in controlled trials and does not give dosing recommendations for that pairing.[1] Do not stack them from a blog.

### Is Lyrica an opioid?

No. It is an anticonvulsant. It is still a US Schedule V controlled substance because of abuse and dependence findings, not because it is morphine.[1][3][4]

### Is gabapentin a US controlled substance?

Federally, no. The Neurontin label lists no DEA schedule.[2] Some states schedule it as Schedule V or require PDMP reporting. Michigan scheduled it and later rescinded that policy.[9] Pregabalin is Schedule V in every US state because the federal listing applies everywhere.[3]

### Does 75 mg pregabalin equal 300 mg gabapentin?

No. The 6:1 figure is a pharmacokinetic cartoon that breaks when gabapentin bioavailability is falling.[2][6] This catalog only lists 300 mg pregabalin anyway.

### Why is Lyrica scheduled if Neurontin is not?

Pregabalin went through later abuse-liability testing and was placed in Schedule V in 2005.[3] Gabapentin was approved in 1993 and remains unscheduled at the federal level.[2] The UK later put both in Class C.[8] That is a timeline of the trials. It is not proof that gabapentin cannot be misused.
