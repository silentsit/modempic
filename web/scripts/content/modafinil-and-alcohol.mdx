Should you mix modafinil and alcohol? The [Provigil label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e16c26ad-7bc2-d155-3a5d-da83ad6492c8) is blunt: the combination has not been studied, and patients should be advised that it is prudent to avoid alcohol while taking the drug.[1]

That is a counseling line, not a pharmacokinetic table. I did not find a dedicated human trial that dosed modafinil with beverage alcohol and then reported the interaction as the primary question. Trials that put "modafinil" and "alcohol" in the same title are mostly treatment studies in people with alcohol dependence, run while they were supposed to be abstinent.[2]

This page is catalog and label education, not medical advice. Legal status varies by country.

## What the label actually says

In patient-counseling language, the US prescribing information tells clinicians to advise patients that Provigil plus alcohol has not been studied, and that it is prudent to avoid alcohol during treatment.[1]

The same label lists psychiatric adverse reactions, including anxiety, nervousness, and insomnia at 5% or more in pooled trials, and postmarketing reports of mania, delusions, hallucinations, and aggression.[1] It also warns that the drug is not a substitute for sleep and may not stop all sleepiness.[1] None of those sentences is a green light to drink on a wake-promoting tablet.

The Medication Guide flags a history of alcohol abuse as something to tell the prescriber, because modafinil is Schedule IV and can be abused or lead to dependence.[1]

## The studies are about dependence treatment, not mixing drinks

Commodity pages often cite "research on modafinil and alcohol" as if someone ran a bar-lab PK study. The paper that actually exists is a different question.

Joos and colleagues randomized 83 abstinent adults with alcohol dependence to 10 weeks of modafinil 300 mg/day or placebo.[2] Overall, modafinil did not change the percentage of abstinent days or heavy-drinking days. In a subgroup with poor response inhibition at baseline, time to relapse was longer. In the subgroup with better baseline inhibition, heavy-drinking days went up and abstinent days went down.[2]

That is a signal about impulsivity and relapse in a treatment trial. It is not a mixing-safety study. It is also not a labeled use. Modafinil is not FDA-approved for alcohol use disorder.[1]

Do not read Joos as permission to drink on 200 mg.

## Alcohol still does what alcohol does

[NIAAA](https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/alcohols-effects-health) describes alcohol as a central nervous system depressant that impairs judgment, coordination, and driving, with risk that rises as blood alcohol rises.[3] [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/alcohol.html) covers the same acute impairment and longer-term organ risk.[4]

A wakefulness-promoting label does not claim to reverse those effects.[1] Feeling less sleepy is not the same as being unimpaired.

The Provigil label already warns about driving and machinery if sleepiness persists.[1]

I am not going to invent a "two drinks is fine" cutoff. The label refused to study the pair.

## A wake tablet is not a sobriety test

[CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/impaired-driving/about/index.html) puts the usual US adult legal driving limit at 0.08 g/dL BAC (0.05 in Utah) and notes that impairment starts lower.[5] A wake tablet is not a BAC test. The Provigil driving warning is about leftover sleepiness. It does not cancel an alcohol limit.

## Three people, same question, different file

A night-shift worker on labeled shift-work dosing who wants a beer after the shift still has a ~15-hour effective half-life on board.[1] The counseling line does not carve out "only during the shift."

A well-slept person using off-label modafinil as a weekend focus tablet is already outside the US indications.[1] Adding alcohol does not create an evidence file. It adds a depressant the label told clinicians to advise against.

A patient in recovery from alcohol dependence is the Joos population, not a mixing population.[2] That decision belongs with the clinicians treating the dependence, not a product page.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Can you drink on modafinil?

The label says the combination has not been studied and that it is prudent to avoid alcohol while taking it.[1] That is the regulatory answer. I do not have a mixing RCT that contradicts it.

### Does modafinil cancel alcohol?

No source I used claims that. Alcohol still impairs coordination and judgment.[3][4] Modafinil's labeled job is leftover sleepiness in three adult sleep disorders, and it may not clear all sleepiness.[1]

### Is there a study of modafinil plus alcohol?

Not a dedicated co-administration safety trial in the file I used. The randomized work that names both is treatment of alcohol dependence during intended abstinence, with mixed subgroup results.[2]

### Why does the label mention alcohol at all?

Because clinicians are told to counsel on it, and because the Medication Guide asks about a history of alcohol or drug dependence on a Schedule IV product.[1] Absence of a PK table is the point, not a loophole.

### What about one glass of wine?

The label does not publish a threshold.[1] "Prudent to avoid" is the line they printed.
