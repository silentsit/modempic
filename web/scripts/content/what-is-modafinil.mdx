What is modafinil? A wakefulness-promoting agent, US-labeled for adult excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS) from narcolepsy, obstructive sleep apnea (with airway treatment), or shift work disorder.[1][2][3] Wellness copy still sells it as a non-addictive smart drug.

The Schedule IV file is not a rumor. Donald Jasinski gave stimulant-experienced men placebo, methylphenidate, and modafinil. They liked modafinil and told it from placebo. The Amphetamine Scale on the Addiction Research Center Inventory did not rise.[5] Nora Volkow's PET group then measured dopamine-transporter occupancy at 200 mg and 400 mg: 53.8% in caudate, 47.2% in putamen, 39.3% in nucleus accumbens, with a dopamine increase in accumbens.[6]

Treat it as a labeled wake drug with a controlled-substance file. This page is catalog and label education, not medical advice. Legal status varies by country.

## Why a "non-addictive" drug is still Schedule IV

The [DEA placed modafinil in Schedule IV](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/1999/01/27/99-1791/schedules-of-controlled-substances-placement-of-modafinil-into-schedule-iv) on 27 January 1999 because it had accepted medical use and a lower abuse potential than Schedule III drugs, not because reinforcement was zero.[7]

Jasinski ran the human test at Johns Hopkins Bayview. Twenty-four men with polysubstance abuse that included cocaine stayed as inpatients. Each man got placebo, methylphenidate 45 mg and 90 mg, and modafinil 200 mg, 400 mg, and 800 mg, then 72 hours of ratings after every dose.[5] Cephalon paid for the whole study.

They discriminated both drugs from placebo. They liked both. Modafinil 400 mg and 800 mg moved "feel the drug," "like the drug," and "high now." The 200 mg dose moved "like the drug" only.[5]

The Amphetamine Scale is where the profiles split. Both methylphenidate doses raised it. No modafinil dose did.[5]

A planned 4-point bioassay of modafinil 400/800 mg against methylphenidate 45/90 mg failed validity. Mean methylphenidate responses were higher. The drugs were not interchangeable on those scales.[5]

Observers thought subjects felt and liked methylphenidate more than any modafinil dose. Only 800 mg modafinil produced a significant "stimulant" identification, and that rating stayed below methylphenidate.[5]

Sleep suppression was stronger at 800 mg than at the labeled 200 mg pairing. Orthostatic tachycardia and the drop in lunch calories were smaller on modafinil than on methylphenidate at 200 mg and 400 mg.

Warot's group saw the same shape in healthy volunteers. 300 mg modafinil sat closer to 300 mg caffeine than to 15 mg dextroamphetamine on subjective scales. Asked which drug they would take again, they picked amphetamine.[8]

Volkow's 2009 PET study is the imaging half. Ten healthy men took 200 mg or 400 mg at Brookhaven. [11C]cocaine binding fell in striatum (DAT occupancy). [11C]raclopride binding fell too, which the authors read as more extracellular dopamine, including in nucleus accumbens.[6] Occupancy tracked the plasma level.

The paper's close is blunt. Drugs that raise dopamine in accumbens can be abused. The "almost no abuse liability" line needed a correction.

Schedule IV is that statutory correction. It is still not Schedule II. Import and prescription rules differ by country.

## The mechanism the label still will not name

The [Provigil label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e16c26ad-7bc2-d155-3a5d-da83ad6492c8) states that modafinil's mechanism of action for promoting wakefulness is unknown.[1] StatPearls classifies it as a non-amphetamine central nervous system stimulant with wakefulness-promoting properties — a classification, not an explanation.[11]

Blog copy treats the "unknown" line as outdated. It lists dopamine, norepinephrine, serotonin, histamine, orexin, glutamate, and GABA as if the circuit were finished. Battleday and Brem still have to call those systems associated changes, not a proven causal chain.[9]

Volkow's DAT occupancy at labeled-range oral doses is the most concrete human number we have.[6] It does not rewrite the label into a dopamine-reuptake monograph.

Wake-promoting actions on the label resemble some sympathomimetic agents, including amphetamine and methylphenidate. The pharmacologic profile is not identical.[1]

Read those neurotransmitter lists as candidate maps. They are a poor user manual for stacking caffeine, Adderall, or a second eugeroic.

## The smart-drug claim against the trial set

In healthy adults who are not sleep-deprived, measured cognitive gains are small and task-specific.[9][10]

[Battleday and Brem](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euroneuro.2015.07.028) pulled 24 randomized, placebo-controlled English-language studies from 1990 through 2014.[9] They also flagged a methods problem: many of the tests were built to catch deficits in ill patients, so a healthy sample can look like a null.

On simple tests, executive function often moved. Attention or learning moved in about half the papers. A few studies reported worse divergent creative thinking. Complex batteries moved attention, executive function, and learning more often. Mood and side-effect reports did not pile up past placebo in most of the set.

Roberts, Jones, Sumnall, Gage, and Montgomery later pooled 14 healthy-adult modafinil studies (64 effect sizes).[10] Overall standardized mean difference: 0.12. Memory updating, the one domain that passed their subgroup cut, was 0.28.

Those are small lab effects. They do not look like a 14-hour desk day.

Sleep-deprived samples show larger wakefulness gains. That is closer to the labeled job.[1][2] A well-slept person doing rote recall is not that sample.

## Three people, same tablet, different evidence

The same 200 mg tablet sits on three different evidence piles.

A night-shift nurse on a 19:00 to 07:00 rotation is inside the shift-work indication if a prescriber has made that diagnosis. The label times the usual 200 mg dose about one hour before the shift.[1] Peak plasma is about 2 to 4 hours later. Food can push peak about an hour.[1]

Effective half-life after repeated doses is about 15 hours.[1] Dose at 18:00 for a 19:00 start and a large fraction of the curve is still present when that nurse tries to sleep at 08:00.

That is a clock problem.

An OSA patient who still nods at 14:00 on CPAP is also inside a labeled use, with a hard limit. Modafinil treats leftover sleepiness. It does not open the airway. It does not replace CPAP.[1][2][3] People who stop the mask because they "feel awake" are fighting the indication.

A well-slept analyst who wants a cleaner afternoon of spreadsheet work has no FDA indication.[1] That person is asking Battleday and Roberts to do a job those papers only half support: small, task-bound bumps, sometimes a hit to open-ended creative work, and no labeled dose for "focus."[9][10]

That is the group most exposed to smart-drug copy.

## Reading the label like a pharmacist

Labeled adult timing is once in the morning for narcolepsy or OSA, or about one hour before a night shift for shift-work disorder.[1] The typical labeled adult dosage is 200 mg. Some trial arms used 400 mg. Severe hepatic impairment gets a reduced dose on the label.[1]

Tmax is about 2 to 4 hours. Food can delay peak by about an hour.[1] Effective elimination half-life after multiple doses is about 15 hours.[1]

The drug is not a substitute for sleep. The label says it may help sleepiness from the listed disorders, will not cure them, and may not stop all sleepiness.[1][3]

Modafinil induces CYP3A4/5 and inhibits CYP2C19.[1] Hormonal contraceptives can fail. The label wants a backup method during treatment and for one month after stopping.[1] CYP2C19 substrates named on the label include warfarin, diazepam, phenytoin, propranolol, omeprazole, and clomipramine.[1]

Serious rash, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome and DRESS, is a stop-and-evaluate event unless the prescriber is sure the rash is unrelated.[1] Pediatric modafinil trials in ADHD showed a serious rash signal, including possible SJS. Modafinil is not approved for pediatric use for any indication. It is not approved for ADHD in adults either.[1]

Common adverse reactions at 5% or more on the Provigil label: headache, nausea, nervousness, rhinitis, diarrhea, back pain, anxiety, insomnia, dizziness, dyspepsia.[1] Discontinuations from adverse events were 8% on modafinil versus 3% on placebo in the pooled trials. Headache was the most common reason people stopped.[1]

In the US it is Schedule IV. Physicians are told to watch patients with a history of stimulant abuse.[1][7] Other countries write different rules.

Modalert, Modvigil, Modaheal, Modactive, and the other 200 mg names on a generic invoice are racemic modafinil. The US reference brand on the label is Provigil.[1] Different trade dress is not a different molecule.

## Questions people actually type

### Is modafinil addictive?

It has abuse potential on the US label and a Schedule IV listing.[1][7] Jasinski's stimulant-experienced subjects liked 400 mg and 800 mg without an Amphetamine Scale rise.[5] Volkow showed DAT occupancy and an accumbens dopamine increase at 200 mg and 400 mg.[6] "Non-addictive" oversells that file.

### Is modafinil a controlled substance?

Yes. The DEA placed it in Schedule IV in 1999, citing accepted medical use and abuse potential lower than Schedule III drugs.[7] Human abuse-liability testing found real, smaller-than-methylphenidate reinforcing effects, not zero.[5][6] It is not Schedule I or II.

### What are the most common modafinil side effects?

Headache, nausea, nervousness, rhinitis, diarrhea, back pain, anxiety, insomnia, dizziness, and dyspepsia at 5% or more on the Provigil label.[1] Headache was the most common reason people stopped taking it in the pooled trials.[1]

### Does it cause a crash?

The label does not describe an amphetamine-style crash. It does warn that the drug is not a substitute for sleep and may not clear all sleepiness.[1] A 15-hour effective half-life means a late dose can still be on board at bedtime. Leftover fatigue the next morning is often sleep debt. It is not a documented rebound syndrome.

### Can you build tolerance with daily use?

The narcolepsy and OSA labels assume repeated daily dosing and quote a ~15-hour half-life after multiple doses.[1] They do not publish a clean "tolerance week" for healthy off-label use. If a prescribed course stops working, ask the prescriber. Do not titrate from a blog.

### Does it help ADHD?

No FDA approval for ADHD in any age group.[1] Pediatric ADHD trials carried a serious rash signal, including possible SJS, and pediatric use is not approved for any indication.[1] Off-label adult ADHD prescribing happens. It is not a labeled use. This page will not dose it.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What is modafinil used for on the US label?

Adult excessive sleepiness from narcolepsy, obstructive sleep apnea (with airway treatment still in place), or shift work disorder.[1][2][3] It is not approved as a general productivity drug.

### How long does modafinil last?

Effective elimination half-life after multiple doses is about 15 hours.[1] Peak plasma is about 2 to 4 hours after a dose. Food can delay peak by about an hour.[1]

### Is modafinil a stimulant?

The label groups wake-promoting actions with some sympathomimetic agents but says the pharmacologic profile is not identical to amphetamine or methylphenidate.[1] Jasinski's ARCI Amphetamine Scale stayed flat across 200 mg, 400 mg, and 800 mg.[5]

### Is modafinil legal to buy?

In the US it is a Schedule IV prescription drug.[1][7] Other countries set their own import and prescription rules. Do not treat a product page as a legal opinion for your address.

### What is the usual labeled adult dose?

200 mg once in the morning for narcolepsy or OSA, or about one hour before the night shift for shift-work disorder, with a lower dose in severe hepatic impairment.[1] This page does not personalize a dose.
