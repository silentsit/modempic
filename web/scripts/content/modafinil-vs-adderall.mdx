Modafinil vs Adderall names two different stimulants, not two flavors of the same one. Modafinil ([Provigil](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e16c26ad-7bc2-d155-3a5d-da83ad6492c8)) is a Schedule IV wakefulness-promoting agent FDA-approved for narcolepsy, obstructive sleep apnea, and shift work disorder.[1] Adderall ([mixed amphetamine salts](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f22635fe-821d-4cde-aa12-419f8b53db81)) is a Schedule II CNS stimulant FDA-approved for ADHD and, in its immediate-release form, narcolepsy.[2][3]

The DEA schedule gap looks decisive. Schedule II means high abuse potential, Schedule IV means low.[4] But the one randomized trial that has actually pitted these two drugs against each other head to head found the gap narrower than that schedule difference suggests. On some measures, Adderall's active ingredients won.[5]

This page compares the two on their labels, the trial data, and the ADHD evidence, not on which one "feels" gentler. It is catalog and label education, not medical advice. Legal status varies by country.

## The comparison, at a glance

| Attribute | Modafinil (Provigil) | Adderall (mixed amphetamine salts) |
|---|---|---|
| Drug class | Wakefulness-promoting agent (eugeroic) | Amphetamine-class CNS stimulant |
| DEA schedule | Schedule IV[6] | Schedule II[2][4] |
| FDA-approved uses | Narcolepsy, OSA (adjunct to CPAP), shift work disorder[1] | ADHD (IR: ages 3+; XR: ages 6+); narcolepsy (IR only, ages 6+)[2][3] |
| Not FDA-approved for | ADHD, at any age[1] | Off-label cognitive enhancement in healthy adults |
| Typical adult dose | 200 mg once daily[1] | IR: up to 40 mg/day for ADHD, 5–60 mg/day for narcolepsy; XR: 20 mg once daily[2][3] |
| Elimination half-life | ~15 hours[1] | d-amphetamine ~10 hours; l-amphetamine ~13 hours (adults)[3] |
| Refill rules (US) | Up to 5 refills in 6 months, per state law | No refills on a single prescription; new script each time |

Modafinil is not approved for ADHD at any dose, in any country label reviewed here.[1] Adderall XR carries no narcolepsy indication. Only the older immediate-release Adderall does.[2][3] Most "modafinil vs Adderall for ADHD" comparisons skip that XR/IR split.

## Same abuse-liability logic, different number

The DEA schedules a substance on three factors: abuse potential, accepted medical use, and how much dependence abuse produces relative to the schedule below it.[4] Schedule II substances have "high potential for abuse" and can produce severe dependence. Schedule IV substances have "low potential for abuse relative to schedule III" and produce comparatively limited dependence.[4]

The DEA's own schedule list names Adderall directly as a Schedule II stimulant, in the same tier as methamphetamine and methylphenidate.[4] Modafinil moved to Schedule IV in 1999, in the same tier as diazepam and alprazolam, after the DEA decided its abuse potential was real but lower than Schedule III drugs.[6]

That is a real difference in law and in reinforcement studies. It is not the same claim as "modafinil is a mild stimulant and Adderall is a strong one." Read the trial below before assuming the schedule gap maps cleanly onto clinical effect.

## The trial that actually compares them

Most modafinil-vs-Adderall content states that no head-to-head trial exists. One does. [Trotti and colleagues at Emory](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40263-024-01122-y) ran a blinded, randomized, non-inferiority trial of modafinil against amphetamine-dextroamphetamine, Adderall's two active salts, in adults with idiopathic hypersomnia or narcolepsy type 2.[5]

Forty-four adults were titrated individually up to modafinil 200 mg twice daily or amphetamine-dextroamphetamine 20 mg twice daily, then tracked for 12 weeks. The primary outcome was change in the Epworth Sleepiness Scale.[5]

On that primary outcome, modafinil kept its edge: an average 5.0-point improvement versus 4.4 points for amphetamine-dextroamphetamine. The difference did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.64). But amphetamine-dextroamphetamine also did not statistically prove non-inferiority (p = 0.11); the confidence interval could not rule out that modafinil was better.[5]

On several secondary measures, amphetamine-dextroamphetamine won or matched. It was non-inferior to modafinil on overall disease severity, sleep inertia, and the Hypersomnia Severity Index. Its "much or very much improved" response rate on cognitive symptoms was more than double modafinil's: 50% versus 22.7%.[5]

Adverse events cut the other way from the "modafinil is gentler" script. Nearly one in three participants on modafinil dropped out from adverse events, versus about one in eleven on amphetamine-dextroamphetamine. That gap was not statistically significant at this sample size (p = 0.13). Both severe adverse events in the trial happened on modafinil, including one psychiatric hospitalization. Anxiety was more common on modafinil; appetite suppression was more common on amphetamine-dextroamphetamine.[5]

This trial studied idiopathic hypersomnia and narcolepsy type 2, at hypersomnia-range doses, not ADHD or off-label focus use in healthy adults. Do not extend a 44-person sleep-disorder trial into a blanket verdict on which drug is "safer" for studying.

## For ADHD, the evidence cuts the other way

Adderall has FDA approval for ADHD. Modafinil does not, in adults or children.[1][2] The evidence base explains why.

A 2018 network meta-analysis pooled 133 randomized trials of ADHD medications.[7] In adults, amphetamines beat placebo on clinician-rated core symptoms (standardized mean difference −0.79). Modafinil did not. Its SMD was 0.16, 95% CI −0.28 to 0.59; the interval crosses zero, so there is no reliable effect.[7]

Tolerability ran the same direction. In adults, modafinil was worse tolerated than placebo, with an odds ratio of 4.01 (95% CI 1.42 to 11.33). That is a bigger tolerability penalty than methylphenidate or atomoxetine showed in the same analysis.[7] In direct head-to-head comparisons across the pooled trials, amphetamines outperformed modafinil on clinician-rated efficacy in both children and adults.[7]

Modafinil did clear placebo on one specific measure: teacher-rated symptoms in children and adolescents (SMD −0.76).[7] That is a real, if narrow, signal. It is not the same as "modafinil works about as well as Adderall for ADHD," a claim several commodity sites make without a source.

## Sleep disorders: modafinil is preferred, not just "safer"

Flip to narcolepsy and idiopathic hypersomnia, and the guideline picture reverses. The [American Academy of Sleep Medicine's 2021 clinical practice guideline](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34743789/) gives modafinil a strong recommendation for both narcolepsy and idiopathic hypersomnia in adults.[8] Dextroamphetamine gets only a conditional recommendation, and only for narcolepsy. It has no idiopathic hypersomnia recommendation at all.[8]

A strong recommendation means clinicians should follow it under most circumstances. A conditional one means the clinician has to weigh the individual patient.[8] That gap exists because amphetamines have a smaller randomized-trial base for these disorders, not because they clearly work worse. The Trotti trial above is part of why that evidence base is starting to grow.[5]

## Three people, same question, different answer

A college student without an ADHD diagnosis wants a study aid. Neither drug has an FDA indication for that. The 2018 network meta-analysis found no reliable modafinil effect over placebo in adults on clinician-rated measures, and Adderall obtained without a prescription carries both legal risk and Schedule II abuse liability.[2][4][7] This is off-label use of a controlled substance either way.

An adult with diagnosed ADHD is choosing a first medication. Adderall has the approval and the larger effect size in the network meta-analysis.[2][7] Modafinil is not a labeled alternative. It is sometimes tried off-label when a patient cannot tolerate amphetamines, but the evidence for that substitution is thin.[7]

A patient with narcolepsy or idiopathic hypersomnia is picking a first-line drug. Modafinil is the guideline-preferred option for both conditions.[8] Amphetamine-dextroamphetamine remains a reasonable option; the 2024 trial shows it can match modafinil on several symptom domains. But it carries the Schedule II abuse-liability profile and, in this trial, a numerically higher discontinuation rate.[5]

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Is modafinil safer than Adderall?

It has a lower DEA schedule and a lower abuse-potential rating in law.[2][4][6] It is not automatically gentler in practice: in the one trial that compared them head to head, modafinil had a higher (though not statistically significant) adverse-event dropout rate and both severe adverse events.[5]

### Can modafinil replace Adderall for ADHD?

Not on the label. Modafinil has no FDA approval for ADHD at any age, and a 2018 network meta-analysis found no reliable effect over placebo in adults.[1][7] Some prescribers try it off-label for patients who cannot tolerate amphetamines, but it is not a labeled substitute.

### Is modafinil a controlled substance like Adderall?

Yes, both are federally controlled, at different schedules. Adderall is Schedule II; modafinil is Schedule IV.[2][4][6] Schedule II carries tighter prescribing rules, including no refills.

### What is the main difference in how they work?

Adderall increases dopamine and norepinephrine release broadly through the amphetamine mechanism. Modafinil inhibits dopamine reuptake and affects other systems (histamine, orexin) at a lower ceiling, with the FDA label still calling its exact wakefulness mechanism unknown.[1][2]

### Which one lasts longer?

Modafinil's effective half-life is about 15 hours.[1] Adderall's d-amphetamine half-life is about 10 hours in adults; the extended-release capsule spreads that over the day but does not change the underlying half-life.[3]

### Is there a study comparing modafinil and Adderall directly?

Yes. A 2024 randomized trial in adults with idiopathic hypersomnia and narcolepsy type 2, not ADHD.[5] Modafinil kept its edge on the primary sleepiness measure; amphetamine-dextroamphetamine matched or beat it on cognition, sleep inertia, and overall severity. No trial has directly compared them for ADHD.[5][7]
