Modafinil and armodafinil do the same wake-promoting job on US labels.[1][2] Both treat adult excessive sleepiness from narcolepsy, obstructive sleep apnea (with airway treatment), or shift work disorder.

Armodafinil is not a different drug class. It is modafinil with the S-enantiomer removed, leaving R-modafinil only.

Half-lives line up on the labels: about 15 hours after multiple doses for both.[1][2] The split that shows up in pharmacokinetic work is the afternoon plasma curve, not a separate "stronger drug" story.

After a morning dose, armodafinil keeps higher R-modafinil levels later in the day than racemic modafinil at the usual labeled pairing (200 mg modafinil vs 150 mg armodafinil).[5] That is a clock and chemistry question, not a brand contest.

This page is catalog and label education, not medical advice. Legal status varies by country.

## The useful difference is the curve, not "strength"

Milligram-for-milligram "which is stronger" is the wrong question. US labeling compares 200 mg racemic modafinil to 150 mg armodafinil for the same three indications.[1][2]

The 150 mg tablet is the usual parallel dose. It is not proof that armodafinil wins a potency contest.

Picture a morning dose on an empty stomach. The Provigil label puts peak plasma at about 2-4 hours; food can delay peak by about an hour.[1] The Nuvigil label puts peak near 2 hours when fasted, with food able to delay peak by 2-4 hours.[2]

Both labels carry an effective elimination half-life near 15 hours after repeated dosing.[1][2]

The curves diverge after that shared clock. Racemic modafinil is a 1:1 mix. The Provigil label states that R-modafinil has a half-life about three times that of S-modafinil in adults.[1]

At steady state, trough plasma is roughly 90% R and 10% S.[1] S-modafinil clears faster.

It is not inactive junk at matched concentrations. Darwish and colleagues describe R and S as equipotent when concentrations are equal.[5] The racemate just produces a biphasic profile because S falls away sooner while R persists.

Darwish et al. (2009) compared racemic modafinil and armodafinil head to head. Terminal half-lives were similar, but armodafinil maintained higher late-day R-modafinil concentrations at the labeled dose pairing.[5]

Follow-on PK work supports the same frame: the enantiomer profile shifts afternoon exposure, not a different elimination clock.[6][7]

If you need coverage deep into the evening on one morning dose, that late-day R-modafinil data is the label-adjacent reason a prescriber might lean armodafinil. If you want the racemate's mixed profile, modafinil is the labeled racemic option.

Neither choice should rest on blog claims about "cleaner" stimulation.

The myth that armodafinil is "just stronger modafinil" misses the point.[5] The S-enantiomer in racemic modafinil contributes early exposure, then fades. Armodafinil front-loads R-modafinil and keeps more of it circulating after lunch.

Whether that feels better is individual. The labels do not rank subjective alertness.[1][2]

## What the labels actually say they do

Both drugs are labeled to improve wakefulness in those three adult sleep settings.[1][2][3][4] Neither is approved as a general productivity drug.

Mechanism language stays cautious on the labels. Wake-promoting actions resemble sympathomimetic agents including amphetamine and methylphenidate, but the pharmacologic profile is not identical.[1][2]

The mechanism through which modafinil promotes wakefulness is unknown.[1] Do not read either label as proof of a tidy dopamine-norepinephrine-histamine release story.

For obstructive sleep apnea, both labels treat excessive sleepiness only. They do not replace CPAP or other airway therapy.[1][2]

Patients on CPAP are expected to keep using it.[1][2]

Shift work disorder dosing on the armodafinil label is 150 mg about one hour before the work shift.[2] Modafinil follows the same indication with its own labeled timing.[1]

Hormonal contraceptives may be less reliable because modafinil and armodafinil induce CYP3A4/5.[1][2] The labels recommend alternative or backup contraception during therapy and for one month after stopping.[1]

Both inhibit CYP2C19, which matters for warfarin, diazepam, phenytoin, propranolol, omeprazole, and clomipramine.[1][2]

Severe hepatic impairment calls for dose reduction on both labels.[1][2] Elderly patients may see higher exposure; the Nuvigil label notes roughly 15% higher armodafinil exposure in elderly subjects versus young adults in one study.[2]

Neither drug is a substitute for sleep.[1][2] Both labels state they may help sleepiness from the listed disorders but will not cure them and may not stop all sleepiness.

Serious rash, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome and DRESS, is a class stop-and-evaluate event unless the prescriber is confident the rash is unrelated.[1][2]

The Provigil label also documents serious rash in pediatric modafinil trials, including possible SJS.[1] Modafinil is not approved for pediatric use for any indication.[1]

## Side effects are a class list, not a purity trophy

Armodafinil is not "cleaner" because it contains only the R-enantiomer. Common adverse reactions at 5% or more overlap on both labels.[1][2]

Provigil lists headache, nausea, nervousness, rhinitis, diarrhea, back pain, anxiety, insomnia, dizziness, and dyspepsia.[1] Nuvigil lists headache, nausea, dizziness, and insomnia.[2]

Psychiatric symptoms, cardiovascular caution, and pregnancy warnings sit on both labels.[1][2]

Dose-related headache shows up on both labels.[1][2] Discontinuations from adverse events occurred in 8% of modafinil patients versus 3% on placebo in pooled trials, and in 7% of armodafinil patients versus 4% on placebo.[1][2]

Headache was the most common reason people stopped in both programs.[1][2]

In the US, both are Schedule IV controlled substances with abuse potential noted on the label.[1][2] Physicians are told to watch patients with a history of stimulant abuse.[1][2]

Do not state a single global legal status; import and prescription rules differ by jurisdiction.

Depression, anxiety, and hallucinations appear in postmarketing reports for both drugs.[1][2] That risk list is shared; enantiomer purity does not carve armodafinil out of it.

## Off-label questions people actually type

### Is armodafinil FDA-approved for ADHD?

No. Modafinil is not approved for ADHD either.[1]

Pediatric modafinil trials in ADHD showed a serious rash signal, including possible SJS.[1] Modafinil is not approved for pediatric use for any indication.[1]

### Can you take modafinil and armodafinil together?

That is not a labeled combination. You would stack the same pharmacologic class with overlapping R-modafinil exposure.

A prescriber would need a specific reason; this page will not recommend it.

### Does armodafinil feel like Adderall?

The labels say wake-promoting actions resemble some stimulants, including amphetamine, but the profile is not the same as sympathomimetic amines.[1][2]

That is not a claim that either drug "is Adderall" or that one works better for a given person.

## Reader questions

### Is armodafinil stronger than modafinil?

No universal yes. Labels compare 200 mg racemic modafinil to 150 mg armodafinil for the same indications.[1][2]

Higher late-day R-modafinil levels after armodafinil do not make it a blanket "stronger" drug for every person.[5]

### Does armodafinil last longer than modafinil?

Not on the half-life printed on the labels, which is about 15 hours for both after multiple doses.[1][2]

The difference is higher late-day R-modafinil exposure after armodafinil, not a separate 15-hour vs 10-hour clock.[5]

### Can you take both modafinil and armodafinil?

No labeled combination exists, and overlapping exposure to R-modafinil is the main concern. Do not stack them without a prescriber's explicit plan.

### When do peak levels occur?

Modafinil peak plasma is about 2-4 hours after a dose; food can delay peak by about an hour.[1]

Armodafinil peaks around 2 hours fasted; food can delay peak by 2-4 hours.[2]
