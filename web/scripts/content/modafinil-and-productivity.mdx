Modafinil and productivity do not share a US indication. On the label, no. [Provigil](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e16c26ad-7bc2-d155-3a5d-da83ad6492c8) is approved to improve wakefulness in adults with excessive sleepiness from narcolepsy, obstructive sleep apnea (with airway treatment), or shift work disorder.[1] It is not approved as a general productivity drug, a workplace lifehack, or a healthy-adult focus tablet.

The trial file for well-slept people is small and task-specific. Battleday and Brem's systematic review of 24 randomized healthy-adult studies found executive function often moved on simple tests, attention or learning in about half the papers, and worse divergent creative thinking in a few.[2] Roberts and colleagues later pooled 14 healthy-adult modafinil studies (64 effect sizes) and got an overall standardized mean difference of 0.12. Memory updating, the one domain that cleared their subgroup cut, was 0.28.[3]

Those are laboratory effect sizes. They are not a 14-hour desk day.

This page is catalog and label education, not medical advice. Legal status varies by country.

## What "productivity" is asking for

Search copy treats modafinil as a workplace multiplier: more hours, cleaner focus, fewer mistakes.

The label treats it as a wake drug for three diagnosed adult sleep problems. It may not stop all sleepiness. It is not a substitute for sleep.[1]

If the reader is sleep-deprived because of one of those diagnoses, the relevant file is the indication plus the sleep-loss stimulant comparisons on [modafinil and caffeine](/blog/modafinil-and-caffeine). If the reader is well slept and shopping for output, the relevant file is Battleday and Roberts, not a listicle of seven super benefits.

## The healthy-adult numbers

[Battleday and Brem](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euroneuro.2015.07.028) pulled English-language randomized, placebo-controlled healthy-adult studies from 1990 through 2014.[2] They also flagged a methods problem: many of the tests were built to catch deficits in ill patients, so a healthy sample can look like a null.

On simple tests, executive function often moved. Attention or learning moved in about half the papers. A few studies reported worse divergent creative thinking. Complex batteries moved attention, executive function, and learning more often. Mood and side-effect reports did not pile up past placebo in most of the set.[2]

Roberts, Jones, Sumnall, Gage, and Montgomery pooled 14 healthy-adult modafinil studies.[3] Overall SMD 0.12. Memory updating 0.28. Small lab effects. They do not look like a management-book transformation.

The Provigil label's usual adult dose is 200 mg. Some trial arms used 400 mg. There is no labeled dose for "focus at work."[1]

## The labeled job is leftover sleepiness

A night-shift nurse on a 19:00 to 07:00 rotation with shift work disorder is inside an indication if a prescriber has made that diagnosis. The label times the usual 200 mg dose about one hour before the shift.[1] Peak plasma is about 2 to 4 hours later. Effective half-life after repeated doses is about 15 hours.[1] Dose at 18:00 and a large fraction of the curve is still present when that nurse tries to sleep at 08:00.

An OSA patient who still nods at 14:00 on CPAP is also inside a labeled use, with a hard limit. Modafinil treats leftover sleepiness. It does not open the airway. It does not replace CPAP.[1][4]

Those two people can look "more productive" because they are less sleepy. That is the labeled mechanism of the outcome, not a new workplace indication.

[MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/druginfo/meds/a602016.html) and [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/drugs-supplements/modafinil-oral-route/description/drg-20064870) describe the same three uses in plain language.[4][5] Neither converts the drug into a lifehack.

## Three people, same tablet, different output

The shift worker above is a clock-and-diagnosis problem. Output at 03:00 is a sleepiness outcome.

The OSA patient on CPAP is a leftover-sleepiness problem. Stopping the mask because the afternoon feels cleaner fights the indication.[1]

A well-slept analyst who wants a cleaner spreadsheet afternoon is asking Battleday and Roberts to do a job those papers only half support: small, task-bound bumps, sometimes a hit to open-ended creative work, and no labeled dose for focus.[2][3] That is the group most exposed to "7 super benefits" copy.

Common labeled adverse reactions at 5% or more include headache, nausea, nervousness, anxiety, and insomnia.[1] Headache was the most common reason people stopped in the pooled trials.[1] A productivity story that skips those numbers is advertising.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Does modafinil make you more productive?

It can reduce sleepiness in the three labeled adult disorders.[1] In healthy, non-sleep-deprived adults, measured cognitive gains are small and task-specific.[2][3] That is not the same claim as a workplace multiplier.

### Is modafinil a smart drug?

Wellness copy uses that phrase. The US label does not. The healthy-adult reviews are the file; they do not support a universal cognitive upgrade.[1][2][3]

### How long does a work-day dose last?

Effective elimination half-life after multiple doses is about 15 hours.[1] That is why a late dose can still be on board at bedtime. It is also why "one tablet, all afternoon" is a clock statement, not a proof of output quality.

### Can you take it every workday without a diagnosis?

Off-label daily use is a prescriber decision. This page will not invent a healthy-adult dosing schedule. The narcolepsy and OSA labels assume repeated daily dosing for those diagnoses.[1]
