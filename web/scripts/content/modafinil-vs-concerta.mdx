Modafinil vs Concerta names two different labeled jobs, not two versions of the same tablet. Modafinil ([Provigil](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e16c26ad-7bc2-d155-3a5d-da83ad6492c8)) is a Schedule IV wakefulness-promoting agent labeled for narcolepsy, obstructive sleep apnea, and shift work disorder.[1] Concerta ([methylphenidate hydrochloride extended-release](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=1a88218c-5b18-4220-8f56-526de1a276cd)) is a Schedule II CNS stimulant labeled for ADHD in people aged 6 to 65.[2]

Concerta is not a different molecule from Ritalin. It is methylphenidate in an osmotic extended-release shell (OROS) built to cover a day with one morning dose. Immediate-release Ritalin is the same active drug on a 2-to-3-hour clock.[2][3] The comparison that already covers abuse-liability numbers and the 2019 adult-ADHD meta-analysis is [modafinil vs Ritalin](/blog/modafinil-vs-ritalin). This page is the formulation split: once-daily OROS versus a wake drug that was never approved for ADHD.

This page is catalog and label education, not medical advice. Legal status varies by country.

## The comparison, at a glance

| Attribute | Modafinil (Provigil) | Concerta (OROS methylphenidate) |
|---|---|---|
| Drug class | Wakefulness-promoting agent[1] | CNS stimulant[2] |
| DEA schedule | Schedule IV[1] | Schedule II[2] |
| FDA-approved uses | Narcolepsy, OSA (adjunct to airway treatment), shift work disorder[1] | ADHD, ages 6 to 65[2] |
| Not FDA-approved for | ADHD, at any age[1] | Narcolepsy, shift work, OSA leftover sleepiness |
| Typical labeled adult approach | 200 mg once daily[1] | Once-daily ER tablet; start and titrate on the Concerta label (strengths 18, 27, 36, 54 mg)[2] |
| Time to peak | About 2 to 4 hours[1] | Mean Tmax about 6.8 hours after an 18 mg dose[2] |
| Elimination half-life | About 15 hours after multiple doses[1] | About 3.5 hours for methylphenidate after Concerta; the tablet is built to mimic three IR doses across the day[2] |
| Refill rules (US) | Up to 5 refills in 6 months, per state law | No refills on a single prescription |

Concerta has no US narcolepsy indication.[2] Immediate-release methylphenidate (Ritalin) does.[3]

## Concerta is ADHD hardware, not a narcolepsy label

The Concerta label describes an osmotic-release tablet. After an 18 mg single dose in healthy adults, mean Tmax was 6.8 hours and mean half-life was 3.5 hours.[2] The same table compares that curve to three 5 mg immediate-release methylphenidate doses given every four hours. AUC values sit in the same neighborhood (41.8 vs 38.0 ng·h/mL).[2] The point of OROS is coverage, not a new receptor.

Concerta carries a boxed warning on abuse, misuse, and addiction.[2] Clinicians are told to assess risk before prescribing and to keep watching. That is Schedule II language, not a "smoother Ritalin" slogan.

Modafinil's label still calls the wakefulness mechanism unknown and keeps ADHD off the indication list.[1] Pediatric modafinil ADHD trials carried a serious rash signal. Pediatric use is not approved for any indication.[1]

## The adult ADHD numbers still favor methylphenidate

Stuhec, Lukić, and Locatelli pooled 20 randomized adult-ADHD trials.[4] Methylphenidate produced a moderate effect on symptoms (SMD −0.50, 95% CI −0.58 to −0.41). Modafinil produced no measurable effect (SMD 0.08, 95% CI −0.18 to 0.34).[4]

Cortese's 2018 network meta-analysis reached the same direction: amphetamines and methylphenidate beat placebo on clinician-rated adult symptoms; modafinil did not.[5]

Those papers do not isolate Concerta from other methylphenidate formulations. They do isolate the molecule. If the question is adult ADHD, the labeled and measured option in this pair is Concerta, not modafinil.

## For narcolepsy, Concerta is the wrong catalog

The [2021 AASM hypersomnolence guideline](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34743789/) gives modafinil a strong recommendation for narcolepsy in adults. Methylphenidate gets a conditional recommendation.[6] That methylphenidate line is not a Concerta line. Concerta's US label does not include narcolepsy.[2]

If someone is shopping "modafinil vs Concerta for sleepiness," they are comparing a labeled wake drug to an ADHD ER stimulant that was never approved for that job.

## Three people, same question, different answer

An adult with diagnosed ADHD who wants once-daily coverage is inside Concerta's indication and outside modafinil's.[1][2] The adult meta-analysis is on methylphenidate's side.[4]

A patient with narcolepsy is inside modafinil's label and outside Concerta's.[1][2] Do not treat OROS methylphenidate as a stealth narcolepsy generic.

A student without either diagnosis wants a 12-hour focus tablet. Neither drug is approved for that.[1][2] Unprescribed Concerta is a Schedule II controlled substance.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Is Concerta the same as Ritalin?

Same active drug, different delivery. Concerta is osmotic extended-release methylphenidate meant to replace multiple IR doses.[2] Ritalin IR is labeled for ADHD and narcolepsy and is usually dosed more than once a day.[3]

### Can modafinil replace Concerta for ADHD?

Not on the label, and not on the adult evidence. Modafinil has no FDA ADHD approval. In the 2019 adult meta-analysis, methylphenidate had a real effect and modafinil did not.[1][4]

### Which one lasts longer?

Modafinil's effective half-life is about 15 hours.[1] Concerta's methylphenidate half-life is about 3.5 hours; the OROS tablet is designed to keep plasma in a once-daily, school-day-shaped curve, with a mean peak near 7 hours.[2] Those are different clocks.

### Is Concerta better for narcolepsy than modafinil?

Concerta is not labeled for narcolepsy.[2] Modafinil is, and it carries the AASM strong recommendation for adult narcolepsy.[1][6]

### Is there a head-to-head trial of modafinil versus Concerta?

Not in the file I used. The same-paper ADHD comparison is methylphenidate as a class versus modafinil.[4] The narcolepsy guideline still notes the lack of a direct stimulant-versus-modafinil trial for that indication; details sit on [modafinil vs Ritalin](/blog/modafinil-vs-ritalin).
